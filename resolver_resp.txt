### Synthesis and Final Verdict

#### **Executive Summary:**
The debate centers on the architectural soundness and regulatory compliance of the Aegis-12 cryptographic upgrade, which introduces a tiered "Hot Path / Audit Path" architecture using Ed25519 for fast execution and NIST ML-DSA-65 for post-quantum security. The Critic raises significant concerns about the asynchronous nature of the implementation, leading to vulnerabilities that undermine the system's integrity and regulatory compliance. The Proposer acknowledges these issues and outlines a strategy to address them.

#### **Key Issues Identified:**

1. **Quantum Gap and Deterministic Floor Violation:**
   - **Critic:** The asynchronous implementation of Ed25519 (hot path) and ML-DSA-65 (audit path) creates a "Quantum Gap" where an attacker can spoof the hot path before the PQ proof is generated, violating Article 12's "Deterministic Floor" mandate.
   - **Proposer:** Proposes synchronizing the generation of both signatures to ensure that the PQ proof is verified before any transaction is committed.

2. **Asynchronous State and Evidence Loss:**
   - **Critic:** The state store is vulnerable to race conditions and evidence loss due to its asynchronous and non-idempotent nature.
   - **Proposer:** Plans to implement a robust idempotent event-log system with pre-assigned event IDs and atomic transaction management to ensure consistent and reconcilable states.

3. **Performance Challenges and Resource Management:**
   - **Critic:** The ML-DSA-65 signing process is 10-50x more resource-intensive, leading to potential CPU starvation and self-DDoS risks under high load.
   - **Proposer:** Will adjust resource allocation dynamically and implement PQ-aware backpressure to manage resources more effectively.

4. **Regulatory Compliance and Operational Integrity:**
   - **Critic:** The "FIPS 204 Readiness" claim is misleading and lacks certified compliance, key management, and entropy validation.
   - **Proposer:** Will clarify compliance claims and strengthen key management protocols to align with industry standards.

#### **Final Verdict:**

The Critic's assessment is rigorous and highlights critical architectural flaws that indeed violate the principles of a deterministic floor and regulatory compliance. The Proposer's response demonstrates a willingness to address these issues, but the current implementation is fundamentally flawed and cannot be deployed in its current state without significant architectural changes.
